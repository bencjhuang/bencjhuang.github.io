Open positions

The group launches in August 2026. Recruiting now for graduate and undergraduate researchers.

The CJHuang Group works at the intersection of battery materials, electrochemistry, and synchrotron characterization. Members get hands-on experience designing cells, running beamline experiments at NSRRC and APS, and turning observations into design decisions. The group is small, the projects are real, and members are expected to own them.

PhD Students

Open
Positions 1–2
Start September 2026
Funding Tuition + monthly stipend

What you'll work on

Research projects on solid-state batteries, anode-free Li-metal architectures, or synchrotron-based characterization of battery materials. Beamtime at NSRRC and APS, publications in high-impact journals, and travel support for international conferences are part of the package.

Required

  • BS or MS in chemical engineering, materials science, chemistry, or a related field
  • Strong academic record and demonstrated interest in batteries or energy storage
  • Good English communication; ability to work both independently and in a team

Helpful

  • Hands-on experience with electrochemical testing or materials synthesis
  • Familiarity with characterization techniques (XRD, SEM, spectroscopy)
  • Programming literacy (Python, MATLAB)
  • Prior research experience — undergraduate thesis, summer research, internships

Master's Students

2026 intake

Projects available across solid-state batteries, anode-free Li metal, and advanced characterization. Funding may be available for qualified candidates. Applications go through NTUST's official admission cycles — recommendation-based and entrance examination.

Background we're looking for

  • BS in chemical engineering, materials science, chemistry, or related
  • Genuine interest in energy storage research

Undergraduate Researchers

Year-round

Taiwan Tech undergraduates are welcome to join for independent study, summer research, or senior thesis projects. A good way to get into research before graduate school — you'll be running real experiments, not just observing.

What you'll learn

  • Battery cell assembly and electrochemical testing
  • Materials synthesis and characterization workflows
  • Data analysis and how to write up results
  • Experimental design — picking the right question and the right control

How to apply

Email Prof. Huang at cjhuang@mail.ntust.edu.tw with the materials below. Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis.

What to send

  • CV — education, research experience, publications, relevant skills
  • Cover note — a few sentences on what you're interested in and why this group
  • References (PhD applicants) — names and contacts for 2–3 referees; letters requested if needed
  • Publication list (PhD applicants) — if applicable

Review timeline

Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis. Shortlisted candidates will be contacted for an interview. If you don't hear back within two weeks, please assume the application isn't moving forward at this time.

03 · Context

About Taiwan Tech

National Taiwan University of Science and Technology (NTUST) is a leading technical university in Taipei. The Department of Chemical Engineering offers strong facilities and a collaborative research environment, with a long track record of placing graduates in academia and industry.

Why Taiwan Tech

  • Among Taiwan's leading engineering universities — alongside NTU, NTHU, and NCKU
  • Beamtime access at the National Synchrotron Radiation Research Center (NSRRC) — about an hour's drive from campus, in Hsinchu
  • Strong industry collaborations across battery, semiconductor, and materials
  • Diverse international student community, with particularly strong representation from Southeast Asia

Living in Taipei

Affordable cost of living, an excellent public transit system, food worth showing up for, and one of the safest big cities in the world. English is widely spoken in research environments; Mandarin will help in daily life but isn't required to start.
