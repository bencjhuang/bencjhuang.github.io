What we work on

The Laboratory for Energy Storage Imaging and Spectroscopy (CJHuang Group) bridges fundamental materials science and practical energy storage by designing the next generation of battery chemistries, specifically all-solid-state batteries and anode-free lithium metal batteries.

What sets the work apart is integrating advanced multi-scale characterization with materials development. We use operando spectroscopy and 3D X-ray tomography to see the electrochemical interface as it evolves, turning observations of failure into design rules.

All-solid-state batteries

Electrode and electrolyte architectures for safer, denser cells, with the interface engineering to make them last.

Anode-free lithium metal batteries

No anode material: lithium plates onto the current collector on first charge. We design cells and electrolytes so it plates evenly and strips reversibly.

Operando & in-situ techniques

Watching reactions happen: synchrotron X-ray spectroscopy and diffraction during real cell operation.

3D X-ray tomography

Reconstructing the inside of a working cell: the failure modes, the morphology, the design feedback loop.

Inside a working cell

Top-view X-ray CT slices of a µ-Si anode in an all-solid-state pouch cell, as assembled and after cycling. Drag the divider: evenly packed particles turn into a mud-crack network. Imaging failure like this, non-destructively and in 3D, is how we turn it into design rules. How we image cells →
